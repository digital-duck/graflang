# Concept Book Outline: Cybersecurity

## Chapter 1: Foundations of Cybersecurity

**Key Concepts**
- The CIA triad: confidentiality, integrity, availability, and how they trade off against each other
- Core terminology: threats, vulnerabilities, risks, exploits, and controls
- Threat actors: nation-states, criminal groups, hacktivists, insiders, and opportunistic attackers
- Attack surfaces and the principle of reducing them
- Defense in depth and layered controls
- Least privilege and separation of duties
- Basic cryptography concepts: symmetric vs. asymmetric encryption, hashing, and digital signatures
- Authentication factors and the limits of passwords
- Security frameworks and standards (e.g., NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001)

**Learning Objectives**
- Define the CIA triad and explain how a given scenario affects each property
- Distinguish between threats, vulnerabilities, and risks using concrete examples
- Identify categories of threat actors and their typical motivations
- Explain why defense in depth and least privilege reduce the impact of a single failure
- Describe at a high level how encryption, hashing, and digital signatures protect data
- Locate and summarize the purpose of a major security framework

## Chapter 2: Common Attacks, Defenses, and Incident Response

**Key Concepts**
- Social engineering: phishing, pretexting, and business email compromise
- Malware categories: viruses, worms, ransomware, trojans, and spyware
- Web application vulnerabilities (e.g., injection flaws, cross-site scripting, broken access control)
- Network threats: man-in-the-middle, denial of service, and spoofing
- Vulnerability management: scanning, patching, and prioritization
- Monitoring and detection: logs, intrusion detection systems, and security information and event management (SIEM)
- The incident response lifecycle: preparation, detection, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned
- Security awareness and the human factor
- Ethical and legal considerations in security work, including authorized testing and responsible disclosure

**Learning Objectives**
- Recognize common social engineering and phishing techniques and describe practical countermeasures
- Explain how major malware types spread and how they are detected and contained
- Describe the root cause and mitigation of injection and access control flaws
- Outline the phases of incident response and the purpose of each
- Explain the role of logging and monitoring in detecting intrusions
- Discuss the ethical and legal boundaries of security testing and responsible disclosure
