1. **The "minimum average number of questions" claim is wrong as stated (Key points and "Entropy as a count of questions").** H is a lower bound on the expected number of yes/no questions, not the minimum itself. For a single fair die, the best question scheme averages 16/6 ≈ 2.67 questions (codeword lengths 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3), not 2.58. Shannon's result is that the optimum lies within one bit of H, and approaches H only when many outcomes are coded together. Revise both places to say this, and give the 2.67 figure.
2. **The biased-coin question claim is false (same section).** "Identifying the outcome takes less than one question on average" cannot hold for a single flip, since one question is always needed. Entropy 0.47 bits is reached only by coding long sequences of flips together. The sentence about often needing no question to *predict* the next flip is fine and should be kept separate from identification.
3. **"More information than any computer could store" is an overclaim.** About 10¹⁹ molecules with six coordinates each, at 64 bits per number, is roughly 4 × 10²¹ bits, which is a large but not impossible amount of storage. Say instead that tracking that much detail is utterly impractical, or give the arithmetic.
4. **The particle-swap example is confused.** Gas molecules are identical, so swapping two of them produces the same microstate, not a different one. The example needs two microstates that differ in something observable at the fine level (for example, which half of the box each molecule is in) but give the same pressure, volume and temperature.
5. **"The only smooth function with these properties"** needs a precise hypothesis. Additivity plus the product rule give c·log p; continuity or monotonicity is needed to rule out pathological solutions. Say "essentially the only continuous function" or state the assumption.
6. **The zip claim is overgeneralised.** Compressors approach the entropy only for long, statistically regular sources, and the entropy is a floor they cannot beat. Qualify the sentence accordingly.
7. **The English-letters paragraph should say which estimate is used.** The 4.1-bit figure assumes independent letters from single-letter frequencies. If you mention context, give Shannon's own estimate of roughly 1 to 1.5 bits per letter, with its source.
