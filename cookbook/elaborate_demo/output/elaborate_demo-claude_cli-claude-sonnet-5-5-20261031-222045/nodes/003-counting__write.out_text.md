Counting outcomes means figuring out how many different results are possible when you make a series of choices or do something with several possible endings, like how many different outfits you can make from 3 shirts and 2 pairs of pants. The trick is that when choices are independent, you multiply the number of options at each step (3 × 2 = 6 outfits), and when you're picking between separate alternatives, you add them. Getting good at it lets you answer questions like "how many passwords are possible?" or "what are the odds of winning?" without having to list every single possibility by hand.
