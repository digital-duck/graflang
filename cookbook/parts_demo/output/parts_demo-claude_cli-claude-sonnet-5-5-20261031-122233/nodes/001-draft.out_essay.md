AI-assisted education uses tools like adaptive tutors and conversational assistants to tailor learning to each student's pace and needs. It can give instant, patient feedback on writing, code, and problem-solving that a single teacher could not offer to every learner at once. Teachers can offload routine work such as drafting quizzes and grading, which frees time for mentoring and discussion. Still, there are real risks, including inaccurate answers, overreliance that weakens critical thinking, and unequal access to technology. The best results come when AI supplements skilled educators and is paired with clear guidelines that keep students actively thinking for themselves.
