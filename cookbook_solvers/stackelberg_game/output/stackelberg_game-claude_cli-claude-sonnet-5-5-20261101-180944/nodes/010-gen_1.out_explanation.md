**1. Why Low?** (High, High) isn't credible. If A prices High, B's best reply is Low ($52K vs $38K), which leaves A with only $12K. A's "signal quality" logic ignores B's incentive to undercut.

**2. Backward induction**
- B's move: if A is High, B picks Low (52 > 38). If A is Low, B picks Low (22 > 14). Low is B's best reply either way.
- A's move: A anticipates this, so High yields $12K and Low yields $28K. A picks Low.
- SPNE: (Low, Low), with A at $28K and B at $22K.

**3. First-mover advantage:** There is none. Moving first lets A commit, but the commitment gains nothing because B's best reply is Low regardless. A's $28K vs $22K edge comes from the scale advantage in the payoffs, not from moving first. A also can't commit to a threat or reward that would make High pay.

**4. Versus simultaneous Nash:** Going by the payoffs alone (I haven't seen the r110 output), Low is dominant for both players, so the simultaneous Nash is also (Low, Low). The outcome is the same, a prisoner's dilemma. Observability changes the reasoning, not the result.
