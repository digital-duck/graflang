── Business Explanation ────────────────────────────────────
1. **Outcome:** The only Nash equilibrium is (Low, Low): both firms price at $70/mo and each earns $25K/mo. In business terms, a price war settles in at the lower price, with no firm able to gain by changing its price alone.

2. **Why Low:** Low is a dominant strategy. If B prices High, A earns $55K by undercutting versus $40K by matching. If B prices Low, A earns $25K at Low versus only $10K at High. Whatever the rival does, Low pays more, and B reasons the same way, so both end up at Low.

3. **The gap:** Each firm earns $25K instead of $40K, so each loses $15K/mo, or $30K/mo combined. That is the collective cost of individually rational pricing.

4. **Breaking it:** Several mechanisms can work.
- **Reputation and repeated play:** Tit-for-tat or trigger strategies make undercutting costly over time.
- **Contracts:** Price-matching or most-favored-customer clauses remove the incentive to undercut.
- **Differentiation:** Distinct products reduce direct price competition.

Explicit price-fixing is illegal under antitrust law, so regulation works against collusion here rather than for it.
