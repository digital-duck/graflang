# Judge's Statement: What Prevents a Verdict

I cannot name a winner because the two sides never settle the **three empirical questions** that decide the claim. Each side asserts an answer without evidence, or argues a neighboring question.

## 1. Is AI's cognitive reach a break from history or a continuation of it? (Central, unresolved)

- **FOR** treats AI as "electricity or the computer" and infers net creation. It never shows that the mechanism survives when the machine competes in the same domain where displaced workers would retrain.
- **AGAINST** says there is "no obvious landing zone," but earlier computerization also automated cognitive clerical work (typists, bookkeepers, switchboard operators). Employment still adapted.
- **Missing:** an argument about *what the new tasks are* and whether AI can also perform them. FOR must say where new human work comes from if AI keeps absorbing newly created tasks. AGAINST must say why computerization's cognitive displacement doesn't count as precedent.

## 2. Is demand elastic enough to absorb the productivity gains? (The decisive empirical gap)

- **FOR** relies on the ATM/teller story, which is incomplete: teller employment fell after roughly 2010. It also cites radiology, a single occupation.
- **AGAINST** says people won't buy "ten times more legal summaries," but offers no sector data.
- **Missing:** sector-level elasticity estimates for the most exposed occupations (customer support, coding, translation, legal and administrative work). Without them, the central mechanism of FOR and the central objection of AGAINST are both unverified.

## 3. What is the net count, not the gross exposure or gross creation?

- **AGAINST** compares IMF *exposure* (40% and 60%), which includes complemented jobs, with a *gross* count of new roles ("thousands to low millions"). Exposure is not elimination, and the new-role figure is unsourced and omits indirect and induced employment.
- **FOR** cites WEF projections, which are employer surveys, and counts data-center and chip jobs without addressing their low labor intensity or temporary construction employment.
- **Missing:** a like-for-like estimate of jobs displaced against jobs created, including indirect effects, with a stated time horizon.

## 4. The resolution has no time horizon or definition of "jobs"

- Neither side says whether the claim concerns 5, 20, or 50 years. AGAINST's near-term transition-pain evidence and FOR's long-run historical evidence may both be true.
- Neither distinguishes job *count* from job *quality* (wages, security, hours). AGAINST's "precarious" and "temporary" points matter only if quality counts.
- **Clarification needed:** an agreed horizon and an agreed metric.

## 5. Distribution versus aggregate

AGAINST itself concedes jobs may "balance in aggregate" while "the people eliminated are not the people hired." That is a real harm, but it does not contradict the resolution. FOR never addresses it either. They need to agree whether transition costs bear on the claim or are a separate question.

## 6. Early evidence is thin and unreconciled

- FOR's Brynjolfsson study shows productivity gains for incumbent workers. It says nothing about headcount.
- AGAINST's freelance-market studies show falling postings in narrow segments.
- **Missing:** firm-level or labor-market data on whether productivity gains from AI have so far led to hiring or to headcount reduction.

## What Would Let Me Decide

1. **FOR** should show why AI-created tasks stay human-performed, supported by elasticity evidence for at least two heavily exposed sectors.
2. **AGAINST** should give a sourced net estimate (displaced against created, including indirect jobs) and show that elasticity is too low, rather than arguing that transitions are painful.
3. **Both** should agree on a time horizon and a definition of "job" (count only, or count plus quality).
4. **Both** should cite post-2023 labor-market data on hiring and headcount in AI-exposed occupations, rather than analogies and projections.

Without these, the outcome depends on priors about an unobserved future. The 21 to 19 margin reflects only that FOR argued the resolution more directly, not that it showed the claim to be true.
