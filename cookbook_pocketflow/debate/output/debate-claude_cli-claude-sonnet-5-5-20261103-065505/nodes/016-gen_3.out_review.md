# Judgment

**Narrow edge to FOR, with low confidence.** FOR argues the resolution more directly and makes fewer category errors. Neither side comes near proving its case, and the claim is still undecided on the evidence.

## Argument FOR

**Strengths**
- **Coherent mechanism.** Productivity gains lower costs, which raises real income and demand, which creates new work. This is the standard economic account of why technological unemployment has not been permanent.
- **Mostly accurate evidence.** The Autor et al. figure (about 60% of 2018 employment in titles that did not exist in 1940) and the Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond result (about 14% productivity gain, largest for novices) are real and fairly cited.
- **Task-bundle framing.** Treating jobs as bundles of tasks is the right unit of analysis, and it explains why exposure does not equal elimination.

**Weaknesses**
- **Analogy carries the argument.** Point 1 asserts that the past pattern continues without showing it holds when the machine competes for the new tasks as well. That is the real dispute.
- **The ATM example is incomplete.** Teller employment rose for decades but declined after about 2010. The example supports the mechanism only for a period.
- **Productivity is not headcount.** The Brynjolfsson study shows output per worker rising. It says nothing about whether firms hired more or fewer people.
- **Weak ecosystem point.** Data-center and chip jobs are real but small relative to the roles at risk, and much of the construction work is temporary. The WEF figures are employer surveys, not measurements.
- **Entrepreneurship is speculative.** Point 5 has no supporting data.

## Argument AGAINST

**Strengths**
- **Right question on cognition.** Whether AI automates the domain where displaced workers would retrain is the most important structural issue in the debate.
- **Entry-level point.** The loss of junior roles, which hides the destruction of career ladders in aggregate counts, is a real and under-discussed channel.
- **Friction is well grounded.** The China-shock research shows that adjustment is slow and uneven. The citation is accurate and relevant to transition costs.
- **Demand elasticity.** The objection that demand is not unlimited for every service is correct in principle, and it targets the weakest link in FOR's chain.

**Weaknesses**
- **Mismatched comparison.** Point 2 sets IMF *exposure* (40% and 60%, which includes jobs that are complemented rather than replaced) against an unsourced *gross* count of new roles. This is the largest analytical error in either argument.
- **Overstated novelty.** Computerization already automated cognitive clerical work, such as typists, bookkeepers and switchboard operators, and employment adapted. "No obvious landing zone" ignores that precedent.
- **Self-undermining concession.** Point 4 allows that jobs may balance in aggregate while the people hired differ from the people displaced. That is a distributional harm, not a refutation of the resolution, which is about net totals.
- **Burden of proof.** Declaring that the burden lies with FOR does not substitute for a positive case, and AGAINST makes affirmative claims of its own (precarious jobs, margin capture) without data.
- **Thin early evidence.** The freelance-market studies cover narrow segments and are generalized broadly.
- **Unsupported claims.** The assertions that people would not buy more legal summaries, and that gains concentrate in capital owners in a way that suppresses demand, come with no sector data.

## Comparison

| Dimension | FOR | AGAINST |
|---|---|---|
| Logical structure | Clear mechanism, relies on analogy | Strong on risks, but mixes the claim with distribution and quality |
| Evidence accuracy | Accurate but selectively framed | Accurate citations, flawed comparison |
| Responsiveness to the resolution | Direct | Partly shifts to transition costs |
| Persuasiveness | Coherent and historically grounded | Intuitive and vivid, weaker on net accounting |

## What would settle it

1. Sector-level demand elasticities for heavily exposed work such as customer support, coding, translation and legal or administrative tasks.
2. A like-for-like net estimate of displaced against created jobs, including indirect effects, over a stated horizon.
3. Post-2023 hiring and headcount data for AI-exposed occupations.
4. Agreement on the time horizon and on whether "jobs" means count only or count plus quality.

## Bottom line

FOR wins narrowly because its mechanism targets net totals, which is what the claim concerns, while AGAINST's strongest points (entry-level erosion, adjustment friction, a possible break from history) bear on distribution and transition more than on the net count. If the resolution were read to include job quality or a short horizon, AGAINST would be stronger, and the verdict could reverse.
