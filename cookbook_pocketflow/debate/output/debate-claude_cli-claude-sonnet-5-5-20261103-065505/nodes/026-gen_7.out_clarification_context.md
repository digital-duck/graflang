# What prevents a verdict

I lean narrowly to FOR (21 vs 19), but not enough to call a winner. Six unresolved points decide the outcome. Each needs argument or evidence from the next round.

## 1. What the resolution measures
"More jobs than it eliminates" could mean headcount, hours, wages, or job quality. AGAINST's strongest points (entry-level erosion, precarity, reskilling friction) are distributional, and AGAINST partly concedes aggregate balance ("the people eliminated are not the people hired").

**Needed:** Both sides should state whether the claim is about net aggregate count, and over what horizon (5, 10 or 30 years). If AGAINST wants a distributional reading, it must justify that reading from the wording. If not, it must show the aggregate count falls.

## 2. Does the historical base rate transfer to AI?
This is the decisive contested point, and neither side finished the argument.
- **AGAINST** says AI targets cognition, so no refuge remains. It never addresses computerization, which automated clerical and cognitive work (typing, bookkeeping, switchboards) without net job loss.
- **FOR** never says which task categories AI is unlikely to absorb. It also doesn't explain why newly created job types won't be automated too.

**Needed:**
- AGAINST: a mechanism for why AI differs from computerization, such as speed of diffusion, generality, or the ability to automate new tasks as they emerge. Assertion is not enough.
- FOR: a concrete account of where displaced cognitive workers go, and why those roles resist automation.

## 3. Demand elasticity
- FOR relies on "cheaper means more demand" (ATMs). But teller employment fell after about 2010, and FOR doesn't address the reversal.
- AGAINST asserts demand is inelastic ("people don't buy ten times more legal summaries") with no sector data.

**Needed:** Elasticity estimates or observed volume and employment trends for exposed sectors (customer support, legal services, software, translation). One or two cases would do, such as coding or radiology.

## 4. Productivity gains versus headcount
FOR cites a 14% productivity gain as if it implied employment growth. AGAINST assumes firms bank the savings as headcount cuts or margin. Neither shows what firms actually do with the gains.

**Needed:** Evidence on whether firms deploying AI have reduced, held or grown headcount. Firm-level or sector-level hiring data would settle this.

## 5. Scale comparison
- AGAINST sets IMF exposure (about 40% of jobs) against an unsourced gross-creation figure ("thousands to low millions"). Exposure includes jobs that AI complements, so this is not like for like.
- FOR cites WEF projections, which are employer surveys and not measurements. It gives no data that data-center, chip and energy jobs are large or durable, or that solo founders net-hire.

**Needed:**
- Sourced estimates of jobs eliminated (not merely exposed) and jobs created, in comparable units.
- An explicit treatment of exposure versus displacement.
- Durable-employment estimates for the AI infrastructure sector, separating permanent from construction-phase jobs.

## 6. Observed post-2023 evidence
Neither side offers current data, though the debate is happening in late 2026. AGAINST's only observed evidence is narrow (freelance writing and translation postings in the early post-ChatGPT period). FOR offers none.

**Needed:** Recent labor-market data, such as employment and postings in AI-exposed occupations versus less exposed ones, and unemployment or hiring rates for young graduates in exposed fields.

# What would decide it

- **Toward AGAINST:** Evidence of inelastic demand in major exposed sectors, plus falling post-2023 headcount in exposed occupations (especially entry-level), and an argument for why computerization doesn't apply.
- **Toward FOR:** Evidence that exposed sectors have held or grown employment, along with a convincing account of where displaced cognitive work migrates, and an answer to the ATM reversal.
- **If neither side supplies the data above:** The resolution's net-count wording and the historical base rate keep the narrow FOR lean, but it stays low-confidence.
