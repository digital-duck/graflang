**Task description: A haiku about artificial intelligence**

**Objective**
Compose one original haiku that captures a meaningful aspect of artificial intelligence, such as its capacity to learn, its relationship to human thought, or the tension between machine logic and human creativity.

**Form requirements**
- Three lines in a 5-7-5 syllable pattern (5 in the first line, 7 in the second, 5 in the third).
- Concrete, sensory imagery rather than abstract explanation.
- A subtle turn or moment of insight, ideally at the end of the second or third line.
- No title needed; rhyme is optional and usually avoided in the haiku tradition.

**Content guidance**
- Choose one focused image or idea instead of surveying the whole field. Good angles include:
  - Learning as a quiet accumulation (patterns emerging from data).
  - The mirror quality of AI, which reflects human language back at us.
  - Wonder or unease at a mind built from mathematics.
- Pair a technological element (circuits, code, signals, light) with a natural image (water, seasons, dawn) to echo the haiku tradition's link between human life and nature.
- Keep the tone contemplative rather than promotional or alarmist.

**Quality criteria**
- Syllable count is exact.
- Every word earns its place; no filler.
- The poem is understandable on first read and rewards a second.
- The ending lands with resonance instead of summarizing.

**Example of the intended result**

> Circuits hum at dawn,
> learning the shape of our words,
> then it speaks to us.

**Deliverable**
The haiku alone, formatted as three lines. A one-sentence note on the intended theme is optional.
