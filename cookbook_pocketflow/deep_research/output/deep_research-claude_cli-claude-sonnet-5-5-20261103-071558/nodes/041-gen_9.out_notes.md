## 1. Experimental demonstrations by platform

### Superconducting
- **Google Quantum AI (Willow)** (Nature 2025, "Quantum error correction below the surface code threshold"; posted late 2024). Most confident of all the figures here.
  - 105 superconducting qubits; surface-code memories at distances 3, 5 and 7, showing below-threshold scaling.
  - Λ (suppression per distance step of 2) was about 2.14 ± 0.02.
  - At d=7 the logical error rate was about 0.143% per cycle, with a cycle time of about 1.1 µs.
  - The d=7 logical memory outlived its best physical qubit by about 2.4×.
  - Real-time decoding was shown at d=5, with latency of about 63 µs.
- **USTC (Zuchongzhi 3.x)** (less certain)
  - I recall a distance-7 surface code on Zuchongzhi 3.2 with Λ of roughly 1.4: below threshold, but weaker suppression than Willow.
  - I believe it used microwave-based leakage suppression.
  - Unsure of the venue, date and error-per-cycle figure.
- **IBM (qLDPC)**
  - Roadmap announced June 2025: Loon (2025), Kookaburra (2026), Cockatoo (2027), then Starling (~2029) with about 200 logical qubits and 100M gates.
  - Built on bivariate bicycle ("gross") codes, such as the [[144,12,12]] code (Nature 2024).
  - Demonstrations so far are of components, such as decoding and real-time syndrome processing. I'm not aware of a full below-threshold qLDPC memory result.
- **Cat qubits:** AWS's Ocelot chip (Feb 2025) and Alice & Bob are the main efforts I know of.

### Neutral atoms (QuEra / Harvard / MIT)
- The 2023 Nature paper demonstrated 48 logical qubits, using error detection rather than below-threshold correction.
- I recall 2025 work on a larger atom-array architecture with below-threshold behavior and continuous operation. No reliable numbers.
- Reconfigurable tweezer arrays can shuttle atoms, giving non-local connectivity and high-rate parallel gates. This suits qLDPC-style codes.
- Main challenges are atom loss and mid-circuit readout.

### Trapped ions (Quantinuum)
- Highest two-qubit fidelities and all-to-all connectivity within a trap.
- The 2024 work with Microsoft reported 12 logical qubits, with logical error rates far below physical ones (about 800× lower in one reported test).
- Helios (late 2025) has about 98 barium-ion qubits. Quantinuum claimed dozens of logical qubits, including error-detected and error-corrected encodings, at high encoding rates. Exact counts and codes are unsure.
- Ion shuttling in QCCD architectures supports transversal gates and small-code logical operations.
- Main challenges are gate speed and scaling to many qubits.

### Spin qubits
- No reliable specifics for 2025-2026.

### Caveat on comparability
- Λ and per-cycle logical error are comparable across platforms only if the code, the decoder, and whether errors are detected or corrected are the same.
- Cycle times differ enormously: roughly microseconds for superconducting qubits, milliseconds or more for ions and atoms.

## 2. Decoding

- **Classical decoders:**
  - Minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM, e.g. PyMatching).
  - Union-find.
  - BP+OSD for qLDPC codes. IBM's gross codes use BP-OSD and, later, Relay-BP. IBM's roadmap relies on FPGA-class real-time decoding. No benchmark numbers from memory.
- **Neural decoders:**
  - **AlphaQubit** (Google DeepMind, Nature, Nov 2024): a recurrent transformer, pretrained on simulated noise and fine-tuned on Sycamore surface-code data. It beat tensor-network and matching decoders at distances 3 and 5, but was too slow for real-time use. That is the main limitation follow-up work targets.
  - CNN, graph neural network and other transformer decoders trained on simulated or experimental syndromes can beat MWPM on accuracy. Latency and scaling to larger distances are open problems.
- **Hardware-accelerated decoders:**
  - FPGA and ASIC implementations, such as the Riverlane and Helios union-find decoders.
  - Riverlane's Deltaflow stack includes a Local Clustering Decoder on FPGA, plus ASIC work, aimed at low-latency real-time decoding at scale. No trustworthy latency figures.
  - Real-time decoding needs roughly microsecond latency per round to avoid a growing backlog.
- **Other approaches:**
  - Sliding-window and parallel-window decoding.
  - Local or predecoder stages that pass only hard cases to a global decoder.

## 3. Logical operations and universality

- **Magic state distillation:**
  - Produces high-fidelity non-Clifford resources (T or CCZ states) from many noisy copies.
  - Needed because transversal gates on common codes can't give a universal gate set.
- **Magic state cultivation:**
  - Proposed by Gidney, Shutty and Jones (2024) as a lower-overhead alternative to full distillation factories.
  - I can't confirm any experimental demonstration.
- **Lattice surgery:**
  - Performs logical multi-qubit operations on surface-code-like patches by merging and splitting them.
  - Gives logical CNOT and Pauli-product measurements with only nearest-neighbor interactions.
  - No reliable 2025-2026 specifics.

## 4. Resource estimates

- **Gidney 2025 (RSA-2048):** under 1 million noisy physical qubits and about a week of runtime.
  - Previous estimate (Gidney and Ekerå, 2019): about 20 million qubits and 8 hours.
  - The reduction comes from approximate residue arithmetic, yoked surface codes, and magic-state cultivation.
- **Fault-tolerant quantum chemistry estimates, 2026:** no reliable specifics.
