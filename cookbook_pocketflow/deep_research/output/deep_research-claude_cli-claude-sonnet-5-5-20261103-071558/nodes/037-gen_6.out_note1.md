The points below come from my own background knowledge, not from the search. I'm confident about the Google numbers and less sure about the rest, so check them against the sources before relying on them.

**Google Quantum AI, Willow (Nature 2025, "Quantum error correction below the surface code threshold")**
- The chip has 105 superconducting qubits and ran surface-code memories at distances 3, 5 and 7.
- Λ (the error suppression factor per distance step of 2) was about 2.14 ± 0.02.
- At d=7 the logical error rate was about 0.143% per cycle, with a cycle time of about 1.1 µs.
- The d=7 logical memory outlived its best physical qubit by about 2.4×.
- Real-time decoding was shown at d=5, with latency of about 63 µs.

**USTC, Zuchongzhi 3.x (less certain)**
- I recall a distance-7 surface code on Zuchongzhi 3.2 with Λ of roughly 1.4, which would be below threshold but with weaker suppression than Willow.
- I believe it used microwave-based leakage suppression. I'm unsure of the exact venue, date and error-per-cycle figure.

**Quantinuum (trapped ions)**
- The 2024 work with Microsoft reported 12 logical qubits, with logical error rates far below the physical ones (about 800× lower in one reported test).
- Helios (late 2025) has about 98 barium-ion qubits. Quantinuum claimed dozens of logical qubits, including error-detected and error-corrected encodings, at high encoding rates. I'm unsure of the exact counts and codes.

**QuEra / Harvard / MIT (neutral atoms)**
- The 2023 Nature paper demonstrated 48 logical qubits, using error detection rather than below-threshold correction.
- I recall 2025 work on a larger atom-array architecture with below-threshold behavior and continuous operation. I can't give reliable numbers.

**Caveat on comparability**
- Λ and per-cycle logical error are only comparable across platforms when the code, the decoder, and whether errors are detected or corrected are the same. Cycle times also differ enormously: roughly microseconds for superconducting qubits against milliseconds or more for ions and atoms.
