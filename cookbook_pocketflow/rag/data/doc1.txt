Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines the power of large language
models with external knowledge retrieval. Instead of relying solely on information encoded in
model weights during training, RAG systems fetch relevant documents from a knowledge base at
inference time and include them in the prompt as context.

The core advantage of RAG is that it allows models to access up-to-date and domain-specific
information without retraining. A typical RAG pipeline consists of three stages: indexing,
retrieval, and generation. During indexing, documents are chunked and converted into vector
embeddings stored in a vector database. At query time, the user question is embedded and used
to retrieve the most semantically similar chunks. These chunks are then prepended to the prompt
before the LLM generates its response.

RAG is particularly useful in enterprise settings where proprietary data must remain private
and cannot be uploaded to an external model for fine-tuning. It also provides natural
citability, since retrieved passages can be surfaced alongside the answer to support
human verification and trust.
